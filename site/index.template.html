What it does

One assistant for the things you used to ask someone else.

Everything starts with your voice. Every screen can also be used by touch, and your phone’s screen reader works with it.

Mirror: how do I look?

Guides you into frame by voice and vibration, takes the photo, then describes your outfit, hair and colours. It points out a stain, a crease, a missing button or clothes that do not go together, and you can ask follow-up questions.

Read: labels, signs, papers

Point the back camera at writing and hear it read aloud. Reading happens on your phone, so it works with no internet and the picture never leaves the phone.

Look around: what is in front of me?

Describes a room, a street or an object, mentioning anything that could hurt you first, and where things are: on your left, ahead, on your right.

Guide me

Watches ahead and tells you about people and objects, where they are and roughly how far, with vibration that quickens as something gets close. It is an extra pair of eyes, never a replacement for your cane or guide.

Where am I, and get help

Ask where you are and hear the street. Say “I need help” and after a spoken countdown you can cancel, VisionMirror texts your location and a map link to your emergency contact. Nothing is ever sent without your confirmation.

Ask anything

The weather today or tomorrow, who someone is, what something means, the time and date. Answers are short and spoken, and it says so when it is not sure.

Made for Nigeria

A natural Nigerian voice, and descriptions that name gele, agbada, buba and ankara properly. Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo are translated for you; Ibibio is on the way.

Control everything by voice

Make the text bigger, speak slower, turn the brightness up, switch to dark mode, change the voice, or ask for more detail. Say “access granted” and it keeps listening after every answer, so you never have to touch the screen. Say “stop” at any time and it stops.

Honest limits: descriptions come from AI and can be wrong, so treat it as a helpful friend, not a guarantee. It never rates how you look. Guide mode cannot see steps, holes or glass.

How to use it

Learn it in one minute.

You do not need to remember commands. Say what you want, in your own words.

  1. Wake itSay “Adanna” or “Bola” while the app is open, or tap the glowing orb. It says “Listening”, then waits for you.
  2. Ask for what you need“Read this.” “Look around.” “How do I look?” “What is the weather in Abuja?” You can say the name and the request in one breath.
  3. Go hands-freeSay “Adanna, access granted.” She keeps listening after each answer. Say “that’s all” to hand control back.
  4. Stop or pause any timeSay “stop” or “pause” while it talks or scans. Say “go on” to continue. On a camera screen, tap anywhere to pause and talk.

Our story

It began with one small question.

How does someone who cannot see check their reflection before they step out of the door? Whether the shirt is buttoned wrong, whether there is a stain, whether the colours work together.

So the first VisionMirror was simply a talking mirror: hold the phone at arm’s length, be guided into frame by voice and vibration, and be told honestly how you look.

Then it grew, the way real tools do: when you can ask for one thing out loud, you want to ask for everything. So Adanna learned to read a label, describe a room, check the weather, tell you where you are, and text your location when you need help. Bola joined so you could choose a voice that feels like yours.

It is built around Nigerian voices and languages, because technology that sounds like home is easier to trust. And it is built to be honest about its limits: it can be wrong, it will say when it is unsure, and it never replaces your cane, your guide or your own judgement.

An assistant that works with your voice, so your hands and your eyes are free to do the rest.

Trust

Your privacy, plainly.

Photos are not kept

A photo is held for a few minutes only so you can ask questions about it, then deleted. You can delete it instantly. Location and phone details are removed from every photo.

Listening is limited

The wake word listens only while the app is open on your screen, and stops when you leave. Your phone’s speech service handles the audio.

You stay in control

Emergency texts are never sent without your voice confirmation, or a countdown you can cancel. An account is optional; guests can use everything.

Questions

Quick answers.

Is it free?

Yes. There is no charge to download or use it.

Does it need the internet?

Reading text works without it. Describing photos, answering questions, the weather, translation and the natural voice need a connection. When you are offline, the phone’s own voice takes over so you are never left in silence.

Which languages does it speak?

Nigerian English, and translations into Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo. Ibibio is on the way.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. VisionMirror is for Android phones for now.

Do I need an account?

No. You can use everything as a guest. An account only remembers your email so your sign-in carries across devices. Someone helping you can set it up with their email.

Can I rely on it to walk safely?

No. Guide mode is an extra pair of eyes. It cannot see steps, holes, kerbs or glass, and its distances are estimates. Always keep using your cane or guide.
